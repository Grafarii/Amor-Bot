## Title
Virtual Dysphoria: A Non-Diagnostic Construct Proposal on Loss of Belonging to the Physical World After Prolonged Immersive Social Virtual Reality Use

## Abstract
Social virtual reality platforms such as VRChat allow users to spend many hours daily embodied as diverse avatars, experiencing gender expression, non-human bodily forms, and community belonging in simulated worlds. Some heavy users report distress when returning to the physical world: the offline environment feels alien, the body mismatched, and the virtual community like home. This article proposes virtual dysphoria as a non-diagnostic, descriptive construct for this technology-mediated discomfort. Virtual dysphoria is not clinical gender dysphoria and does not pathologize transgender identity, furry culture, or exploratory avatar use. It names normative subjective distress centered on loss of belonging to the physical world, affective discordance between the self experienced in immersive VR and the offline physical self, and discomfort at the virtual-to-physical transition after prolonged immersive social VR use. Three proposed dimensions are belonging dysphoria (the physical world no longer feels like home), gender-related virtual-physical discordance, and bodily form or species-related virtual-physical discordance. Grounded in research on avatar embodiment, identity disjunction in social VR, depersonalization and derealization after VR exposure, and prolonged immersive use, the proposal distinguishes virtual dysphoria from gender euphoria in affirming contexts, clinical depersonalization disorders, and virtual confabulation. Implications for psychology, platform design, and public health are discussed, along with methodological guidelines for empirical operationalization.
